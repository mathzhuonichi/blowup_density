\begin{abstract}
We study the density of smooth external forces for which the three-dimensional
Navier--Stokes equations lose classical regularity by a prescribed time $T>0$.
Starting from the compact forced blowup solution of OpenAI, we construct a
singular solution near every regular reference solution while preserving its
initial velocity. A smooth cutoff of a local vector potential makes the
reference velocity vanish near the support of a rescaled blowup solution.
The two velocities then have no nonlinear interaction, and the resulting
force remains smooth through the blowup time. For fixed viscosity and zero
initial velocity, such forces are dense in the relative $L^1_tH^s_x$ topology
on both $\TT$ and $\R^3$ exactly when $s<1/2$. On $\R^3$, the corresponding
$L^2_tH^s_x$ threshold is $s<-1/2$. Density below these thresholds holds for
every fixed admissible smooth initial velocity. Separate critical estimates
give the regular neighborhoods needed for non-density. We also obtain strong
approximation of regular reference velocities in energy and dissipation.
On $\R^3$, smooth compact forces producing breakdown by $T$ are dense in
the completed force spaces $L^q_tH^s_x$ for $q\in\{1,2\}$ and
$s<2/q-3/2$, and in
$L^2_t\dot H^{-1}_x$, for every fixed admissible smooth initial velocity.
Around any whole-space reference regular through $T$, velocity and force
cell averages can be preserved for every $0\le t<T$ on any prescribed
finite family of Cartesian grids.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} forced Navier--Stokes equations; finite-time blowup;
force-space density; Sobolev spaces; compact support.
