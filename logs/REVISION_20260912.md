## Proof order

1. Define the equation, solution classes, force topologies, and maximal lifespan.
   State the shared local existence and continuation result, proved in Appendix A.
2. State precisely the external compact blowup theorem and derive initial
   vanishing and finite dissipation from the energy identity.
3. On the torus, prove localization of fractional norms and parabolic scaling.
   Construct a local vector potential, choose smooth cutoffs, cancel the reference
   velocity near the support of the inserted solution, and estimate the force
   correction. Verify exact cancellation of both nonlinear interaction terms.
4. Deduce density below the critical order. Prove a separate critical estimate,
   remove the periodic mean, obtain the H1 estimate, and invoke continuation.
   Then collect trajectory, mixed-norm, projection, and additional constructions.
5. In the whole space, reuse the local construction. Prove negative-order scaling
   with its explicit integrability restriction, handle lower orders by monotonicity,
   and prove the two critical regularity estimates with separate low-frequency
   energy control. Deduce the sharp thresholds, the compact and rapid-decay
   variants, density in completed force spaces, and equality of grid averages.
6. Keep a single proof of the critical Sobolev embeddings in Appendix B.

## Equivalence requirements

- Viscosity remains fixed under the parabolic rescaling.
- Density means breakdown by T. Exact blowup at T is asserted for insertion into
  a reference that is regular through T, not for arbitrary reference forces.
- Positive density preserves every fixed admissible smooth initial velocity.
  The if-and-only-if density classifications are for zero initial velocity.
- Whole-space L2-in-time non-density is a finite-horizon conclusion, with a
  horizon-dependent radius. No homogeneous negative norm is substituted for the
  inhomogeneous force metric.
- The force is smooth through the singular time and defined on the full positive
  time axis. It is not obtained by extending the singular velocity through T.
- The vector potential, rather than multiplication of the velocity alone, is
  cut off to preserve incompressibility. The smooth Urysohn lemma supplies only
  the cutoff functions; it does not prove the nonlinear cancellation.
- All original secondary results remain present. Shared lemmas are proved once
  and cited in both domain arguments.
- Source theorem verification is distinct from proving the external construction
  or formally certifying this merged article.
